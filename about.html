Personal introduction

I am Tanatswa Magwenya, a third-year BSc Computer Science student at DMI St John the Baptist University in Mangochi, Malawi. My interest in technology began with a simple question: how does this actually work? That curiosity has grown into a genuine passion for programming and the way well-designed software can make everyday tasks easier.

What I enjoy most about Computer Science is problem solving — taking a messy, real-world requirement and breaking it into small, logical pieces until it becomes a working application. I like writing code that is readable, interfaces that are clear, and systems that do something genuinely useful.
I am currently strengthening my foundations in software development, databases and web technologies while exploring artificial intelligence.

Outside of coursework, I learn by building. Each project in my portfolio started as an attempt to understand a concept properly — from a client–server chat application to a desktop tool and a recommendation system. I believe the best way to learn technology is to use it.

Career aspirations

My goal is to become a software engineer who builds reliable, accessible systems that solve problems in my community and beyond. I am especially interested in the intersection of software development, networking and cybersecurity, and I want to keep exploring artificial intelligence as those fields continue to grow.

In the long term, I would like to use technology to improve access to education, healthcare information and digital services in Malawi and across Africa — starting by learning deeply, contributing to real projects, and sharing what I learn with others.

  1. Graduate with a strong BSc Computer Science degree and a portfolio that reflects real ability.
  2. Grow as a software engineer through internships, collaboration and production-level code.
  3. Specialise in networking and cybersecurity while staying fluent in modern web development.
  4. Contribute to open-source projects and build tools that address local challenges.
  5. Give back by mentoring younger students who are starting their own technology journeys.
